How to Create a Logo for Your Startup in 5 Minutes

5 min read · Tutorials

When you're building a startup, the logo is rarely the most important thing you'll do that week — but you still need something presentable for your landing page, pitch deck, or social profiles before the "real" branding gets figured out later. Here's a practical, no-nonsense process for getting a solid logo done fast, without sacrificing the essentials.

Step 1: Know your one-sentence positioning (30 seconds)

Before opening any tool, be able to finish this sentence: "We help [audience] do [thing]." A fintech app for freelancers and a fintech app for enterprises should not end up with the same visual tone, even though both are "fintech." This single sentence will guide every choice you make in the next four minutes.

Step 2: Pick a category, not a specific look (30 seconds)

Rather than scrolling endlessly hoping to stumble on "the one," start by browsing a relevant category — Technology, Business, Finance, whatever matches your positioning. Narrowing the field immediately makes the next step faster and less overwhelming.

Step 3: Choose a template based on structure, not final colors (1 minute)

This is the step most people get stuck on. The color and even the icon of a template rarely matter at this stage — you're about to change both. What actually matters is the layout: icon above text, icon beside text, a badge/emblem shape, or a pure wordmark. Pick the structure that fits how you picture your logo being used — a wordmark works great in a text-heavy website nav bar, while an emblem-style badge stands out better as a small app icon.

Step 4: Swap in your name and tagline (1 minute)

Replace the placeholder text with your actual startup name and, if you have one, a short tagline. Keep it short — a name longer than two or three words starts to fight for space in a small logo. If your tagline doesn't add real information, it's fine to leave it off entirely for now; you can always add one later.

Step 5: Set your brand color (1 minute)

Pick one primary color that matches the tone you want (see our guide to logo color psychology if you're unsure). Apply it to the text and, if the template has one, the icon. Resist the urge to use three or four colors at this stage — one strong, consistent color reads as more confident than a rainbow of options.

Step 6: Check it small, then download (1 minute)

Before exporting, glance at the logo at a small size — imagine it as a browser tab icon or an app avatar. If any text or detail disappears at that size, simplify before you finalize. Once it looks right, download a transparent PNG for digital use (website, app icon, social profiles) and you're done.

What to skip for now

At the five-minute stage, don't worry about:

  • Print-ready or vector files — you likely don't need them yet
  • A full brand guideline document
  • Getting every pixel "perfect" — a good-enough logo you actually ship beats a perfect one still sitting in drafts

You can revisit and refine the logo later once you have real users, real feedback, and a clearer sense of what the brand actually stands for. Plenty of well-known companies significantly reworked their first logo once the business found its footing — shipping something functional now doesn't lock you in forever.
